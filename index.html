Our families

Two cultures, one celebration

Mohammed’s family comes from Riyadh, in the heart of Najd. Sana’s family is Lur from Rojhelat (Eastern Kurdistan), whose traditional homeland spans the southern and western Zagros Mountains in Iran, and Turkmen from Kirkuk in Iraq.

Riyadh, Saudi Arabia

Najd

  • Al-Sadu weaving

    Bedouin women weave Al-Sadu on ground looms from camel, goat and sheep hair. Its red, black and ivory bands run along the edges of this page.

  • Gahwa and dates

    Arabic coffee, lightly roasted with cardamom, poured into small finjan cups and served with dates. It is the first gesture of welcome in a Najdi home.

  • Al-Ardah

    The Najdi sword dance: two rows of men moving to drums and sung poetry, performed at weddings and national celebrations.

Rojhelat and Kirkuk (Iraq)

Lur & Turkmenli

  • Kilim

    Flat-woven rugs in madder red, indigo and green, worked in diamonds and hooked motifs that families pass down.

  • Halparke and govend

    The circle dance at every Kurdish wedding. Everyone links little fingers and follows the leader’s handkerchief, and the circle keeps growing.

  • Turkmenli dress

    Women traditionally wear vibrant red, maroon or burgundy long dresses (koynek) over trousers (balaq), paired with heavy embroidered silk robes called chyrpy and ornate silver and turquoise jewellery.
